Four Icons

Each drawn on Android's 108 × 108 adaptive-icon canvas with the mark kept inside the safe centre, so no launcher shape crops it. The small sizes on the right are the real test — that's how you'll actually see it on your home screen.

01

Control Cross

The universal shorthand for a gamepad — a d-pad and four action buttons, nothing else. Uses all four of the app's real button colours.

Strongest at small sizes. Instantly reads as "controller" with no thinking. The trade-off is that it doesn't say phone — it could be any gamepad app.

Best storyteller, weakest at 32 px. The most self-explanatory of the four and the best choice for a Play Store listing — but the details inside the phone start to mush at the smallest size.

03

P Monogram

A heavy letter P whose counter is a d-pad. The most brandable option — it scales to a favicon, a watermark, or a logo beside the product name.

96 px
72 px

Most grown-up, least descriptive. Looks like a company rather than a hobby project, and works everywhere a logo is needed. But a stranger scrolling the store won't know what it does.

04

Signal

A pad broadcasting upward. Leads with the thing PocketPad is actually better at than its competitors — being wireless and fast.

Says "wireless" loudest. The arcs carry the low-latency promise. Risk: broadcast arcs are heavily used by Wi-Fi, hotspot and podcast apps, so it's the least distinctive shape in a crowded app drawer.
